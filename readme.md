Between 2019 and 2022, I studied, almost obsessively, personal finance. Since then, I've been through 30+ books, countless courses, and an unreasonable number of YouTube hours. It's been quite a ride and, among many things, it gave me the confidence to call BS on snake oil sellers.

Two years ago, I wrote a script out of curiosity: thinking about financial independence, how could I analyze the potential outcomes of a retirement portfolio with annual withdrawals?

Monte Carlo simulation was clearly the way. I assumed annual returns and inflation followed a normal distribution - yeah yeah, I know, I could have used a lognormal or student's t, but simplifying here. I also parameterized the withdrawal rate instead of fixing it at the 4% popularized by that 1994 paper (I personally believe 2.7% is more realistic, link in the comments on why), adjusting withdrawals for inflation in the following years.

Got inspired to build a Streamlit app to give that script a GUI. Check it out, play around, have fun :)
